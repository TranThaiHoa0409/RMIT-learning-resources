## 1. Why Do Ethics & Law Matter in AI?

### Cautionary real-world cases (harms from AI misuse/overuse)
- **"AI psychosis":** In 2024, a Dutch IT consultant lost his marriage and over €100,000 after becoming convinced an AI chatbot was a sentient business partner/romantic interest (Moore, *The Guardian*, 2026).
- The **Human Line Project** (2025) documented 15 suicides, 90 hospitalizations, 6 arrests, and over $1M spent tied to AI-related delusional episodes.
- Stanford research found AI chatbots tend to be highly **sycophantic**, which is especially risky for people vulnerable to delusional thinking (cited across multiple 2025 papers on LLM sycophancy and ethical violations in AI-based mental health support).
- **Generative-AI child sexual exploitation reports** to NCMEC rose sharply: ~4,700 (2023) → ~67,000 (2024) → ~1.5 million (2025).
- Studies also link AI use to increased laziness (68.9%), privacy/security concerns (68.6%), and reduced decision-making ability (27.7%) (Ahmad et al., 2023).
- An MIT study (Kosmyna et al., 2025) found that participants using an LLM for essay writing performed worse than a "brain-only" group across neural, linguistic, and scoring measures after 4 months — described as "cognitive debt."

### Expert warnings
- **Stephen Hawking:** AI "will either be the best thing that's ever happened to us, or... the worst thing... it very well may be the last thing," and warned AI could design self-replicating systems that "outperform humans."
- **Geoffrey Hinton (Nobel laureate):** "It is hard to see how you can prevent the bad actors from using it for bad things."
- Referenced case: Microsoft's **Tay chatbot** incident (rapidly learned to produce offensive content).
- Israel's deployment of AI-powered robotic guns; NYT coverage of Hinton's fears about autonomous "killer robots" as AI systems begin writing and running their own code.

### Deepfake fraud cases
- **2024, Hong Kong:** A finance employee at a multinational was tricked into transferring **$25 million** after a video call where every other "participant," including the CFO, was a deepfake.
- **2026, Singapore:** A business professional lost at least **$4.9 million** in a scam using deepfakes of Singapore PM Lawrence Wong and other officials.
- **"Deepfake-as-a-service"** platforms (e.g., "Haotian") use GANs for real-time face-swapping in live video calls, marketed as easy to use ("one-button operation") and able to respond convincingly to hand-waving/face-pinching to defeat verification checks.
- A fake AI-generated travel ad reportedly tricked a couple into traveling to a nonexistent tourist destination.

### Discussion questions (posed to students)
- *As an AI user*, what will you do? — Stop using AI / Choose ethical AI companies / Learn to use AI safely / Both / Other
- *As an AI developer*, what will you do? — Stop developing AI / Work for ethical AI companies / Make safe AI / Both / Other

## 2. Ethics: Principles & Practical Usage

### Three Main Approaches in Ethics

**Virtue Ethics**
- Emphasizes moral character/virtues — generosity, loving-kindness, courage, compassion.
- Fits "pre-industrial"/simpler societies where material desire was less dominant; prioritizing virtues helped maintain social harmony.

**Deontology**
- Emphasizes rules — an action is right if it follows accepted rules (school rules, company regulations, national laws).
- Enforcing standardized rules helps preserve societal stability.
- Class example: Is running a red light right or wrong? (Complicated further by: what if there's an ambulance behind you? What if an accident happens?)

**Consequentialism**
- One of the most influential moral theories: an action is right if its **result** is good.
- Same red-light example: running it is RIGHT if it saves a life, WRONG if it causes an accident.
- Open problem: How do we determine if a result is good or bad? What if a result is both?

### The Golden Rule
- A near-universal ethical principle, persisting across most human traditions for millennia.
- Endorsed by 143 leaders of the world's major faiths in the 1993 "Declaration Toward a Global Ethic."
- Expressed in many cultural/religious forms, all converging on the same idea — roughly: *treat others as you would want to be treated; do not do to others what you would not want done to you.*

### "Bad for others is bad; good for others is good" — a simplified practical framework
- Examples discussed:
  - Running a red light to let an ambulance pass → judged **good** (helps others)
  - Vietnamese Criminal Code (Article 132): failing to help someone in mortal danger when capable of doing so is a punishable offense — reinforcing a legal duty tied to "harm to others"
  - Price-gouging on medical masks during COVID-19 → **bad** (harms others)
  - A man repairing broken roads with his own money/effort → **good** (helps others)
- General categorization: Killing, stealing, infidelity, lying, drunkenness → bad (harm to others); saving lives, helping those in need, faithfulness, hard work, honesty → good (benefit to others)
- Reference: Martin, K. E. (2015). "Ethical issues in the big data industry." *MIS Quarterly Executive*.

### When outcomes are both good and bad
- Compare impact by **breadth** (how many people affected) and **depth** (how severely an individual is affected).
- **The Trolley Problem** (classic ethics dilemma): You can save 5 people either by (1) switching a lever to redirect a train, killing 1 instead of 5, or (2) pushing one large person off a bridge to stop the train, killing 1 to save 5.
  - Case 1 (lever): Acting saves 5, sacrifices 1; not acting loses 5, saves 1.
  - Case 2 (pushing a person): Same numeric tradeoff, but framed with more moral ambiguity/discomfort (direct physical harm).
  - A well-known aside: a child's own intuitive "solution" to the dilemma (E.J. Masicampo).
- Further example: publicly criticizing philanthropists over alleged charity-fund misuse — **good** (may reduce misuse) but **bad** (excessive pressure could discourage donors, reducing help reaching people in need).
- Closing reflection: ethics frameworks focus on impact "for others" — but what about impact on **oneself**?
