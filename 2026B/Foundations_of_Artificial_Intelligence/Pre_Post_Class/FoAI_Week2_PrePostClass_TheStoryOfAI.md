# The Story of AI

## 1. Origins of AI: The Dartmouth Workshop (1956)

For decades, humanity has been captivated by the idea of machines that can think. The promise of artificial intelligence — machines capable of reasoning, learning, and even creating — has sparked both excitement and skepticism.

In the aftermath of World War II, rapid advancements in computing, cryptography, and automation fuelled a new era of technological ambition. Scientists began asking whether machines could not only calculate but also think. Influenced by cybernetics (how systems regulate themselves) and Alan Turing's theories on machine intelligence, a young mathematician, John McCarthy, believed the time had come to make this question a formal scientific pursuit. In the summer of 1956, he gathered leading minds at Dartmouth College, launching the first official study of Artificial Intelligence.

Though the workshop did not create intelligent machines, it set the foundation for AI research, defining the key challenges that would shape the field for decades to come.

## 2. AI's Waves and Winters

AI's journey has been anything but smooth. Like shifting tides, AI has undergone rapid breakthroughs followed by harsh setbacks when reality failed to meet expectations. These cycles, often called AI's "waves and winters," have shaped its evolution, showing that progress in intelligence — human or artificial — is never a straight path forward.

## 4. AI in Computer Vision: Be My Eyes

Beyond conversational use, AI models such as GPT also power assistive technologies that improve accessibility for people with visual impairments.

The Be My Eyes app was originally designed to connect blind and low-vision users with sighted volunteers for real-time assistance. It has since integrated OpenAI's GPT-4 Turbo with Vision to provide instant, autonomous visual interpretation: users take a picture or use their phone's camera, and the AI describes what it sees — reading labels, navigating unfamiliar environments, or identifying objects.

*Video: demonstration of the Be My Eyes app using GPT-4 Turbo with Vision.*

This AI-powered tool improves accessibility by offering on-demand independence and reducing reliance on volunteers for everyday tasks.

## 5. Case Study: Food Waste and AI Solutions

**The problem.** Approximately 14% of the world's food is lost after harvest (before reaching the market), while another 17% is wasted at the retail and consumer levels — around 30% of all food, or 1.6 billion tonnes, every year. Food loss and waste also contribute 8–10% of global greenhouse gas emissions, accelerating climate change and further threatening global food security. (Sources: [FAO](https://openknowledge.fao.org/items/8f21ef3b-5053-4816-83a6-b13b1a19e66a); [Global Food Waste in 2025](https://greenly.earth/en-gb/blog/industries/global-food-waste-in-2022).)
