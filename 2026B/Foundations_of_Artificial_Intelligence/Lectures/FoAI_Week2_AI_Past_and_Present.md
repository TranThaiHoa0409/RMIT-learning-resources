## 1. History of AI

### Brief History of AI: Three Waves and Two AI Winters

AI development can be summarized as three "waves" of progress separated by two "AI winters":

| Phase | Period | Description | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dartmouth Workshop | 1956 | The formal inception of AI as an academic discipline. | — |
| 1st Wave: Great Expectations | 1950s–1960s | Focused on tasks considered indicative of human intelligence, e.g., games & maths. | Lisp (McCarthy, 1958), Checkers program (Samuel, 1959), Perceptron machines (Rosenblatt, 1957–1962) |
| 1st AI Winter: A Dose of Reality | 1970s | AI was subject to critiques & funding cut-off. Problems: limited computer power, intractable problems, Moravec's paradox. | — |
| 2nd Wave: Expert Systems | 1970s–1980s | Programs that answer questions about a specific domain, using logical rules derived from knowledge of experts. | DENDRAL (Buchanan et al., 1969), MYCIN (Shortliffe et al., 1972), XCON (McDermott, 1978) |
| 2nd AI Winter: Brittle Systems | 1980s–1990s | Expert systems are difficult to build and maintain for complex domains. | — |
| 3rd Wave: Deep Learning | Since 1990s | More data, faster computers, and advanced learning techniques have led to a resurgence of neural networks. | Deep Blue (IBM, 1997), Watson (IBM, 2011), AlphaGo (Google, 2015), ChatGPT (OpenAI, 2022)… |

Further real-world examples of AI for environmental and public safety:

- **AI-enhanced bushfire detection** — In Tasmania, Forico (the state's largest private forest manager) has deployed AI-powered cameras developed by Pano AI across its 173,000 hectares of managed land.
- **Preventing train derailments** — In the United States, the railway industry is using AI to prevent train derailments. Companies like Norfolk Southern have installed automated inspection portals equipped with high-speed cameras and AI software along their tracks, analysing real-time data to detect defects on moving trains and allowing for immediate corrective action.
- **AI-driven typhoon forecasting** — Taiwan, frequently impacted by typhoons, is now using AI to improve storm forecast accuracy. The Central Weather Administration (CWA) has collaborated with tech companies like NVIDIA to implement AI models such as CorrDiff. During Typhoon Koinu last year, the CWA deployed advanced AI models that successfully forecast the storm's trajectory five days in advance.
